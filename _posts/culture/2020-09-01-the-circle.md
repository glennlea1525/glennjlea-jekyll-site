Many years ago I found this poem posted to a wall in a church. I don't know who wrote it but I copied it on to a scrap of paper and kept it all these years. For myself to read...

<div align="center">THE CIRCLE</div>

<div align="center">
When<br>
I first became a<br>
member of the church, my<br>
circle was very big...for it included<br>
all who, like myself had believed and had<br>
been baptized. I was then happy in the thought<br>
that my brethren were many...<br>
<br>
But having a keen and observant mind, I soon learned<br>
that many of my brethren were erring. I could not tolerate<br>
any people within my circle but those who, like myself, were<br>
right on all points of doctrine and practice. Also, some made<br>
mistakes and sinned. What could I do? I had to do something!<br>
<br>
I drew my circle, placing myself and a few as righteous as I within, and<br>
the others without. I soon observed that some within my circle were self<br>
righteous, unforgiving, jealous and proud. So in righteous indignation, I<br>
drew my circle again, leaving the publicans and sinners outside, excluding the<br>
Pharisees in all their pride, with myself and the righteous and humble within.<br>
<br>
Then I heard ugly rumours about some brethren. I saw then that some of<br>
them were worldly minded; their thoughts were constantly on things of a<br>
worldly nature; they drank coffee, when, like me, they should drink tea.<br>
So, duty bound to save my reputation, I drew my circle again, leaving<br>
those reputable, spiritually-minded within. I was surprised to<br>
find that only my family and myself remained in the circle. I<br>
had a good family, but to my disgust, my family finally<br>
disagreed with me. But, I have always been right!<br>
A man must be steadfast.<br>
<br>
I have never been a factious man!<br>
So in my strong determination,<br>
I drew my circle again,<br>
leaving me quite<br>
alone.<br><br>
</div>
