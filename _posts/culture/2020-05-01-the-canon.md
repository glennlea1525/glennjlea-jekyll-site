# {{ page.title }}

I wrote a little poem called *The Canon* during university. A *Canon* means the acceptable, standard literature of a specific genre. English Literature in academia has an accepted list of stories, poems and plays which an English Lit student must read understand Western culture and civilization.

<img  src="{{ baseurl }}/assets/img/blog/norton-anth.png" alt="My dog-eared Norton Anthology of English Literature" style="float: right; margin: 10px; width: 35%" />I had to read and analyze Shakespeare, Milton, Woodsworth, Shelly, Faulkner, and a hundred other famous writers in the Anglo-Saxon world. Thinking about these required readings, I started wondering why I had to read and understand this literature. I asked myself why must I learn this, how does it help me and is it giving me insights into life I didn't know before? Turns out, this literature does provide an explanation to this shared culture we call *Western civilization*. Has it brought us any closer to understanding the "Truth" about life, about how we go about making and maintaining society? <br><br>
Not sure.
<br><br>
Doubtlessly, I know Western Civilization has produced some great literature. Yet this literature has its origins  from in ancient societies. But this literature provides a glimpse into the meaning of life as seen through many windows. It is worth knowing.

For me, a former carpenter turned writer, I saw a glimpse of this shared culture through one book - the *Norton Anthology of English Literature*. I still have my *Norton Anthology*. It is a bit dog-eared. I kept it because it has been well read and it has my *enlightened* annotations next to many of the poems and stories therein.

## The Canon

<p class="excerpt">
<br>Corpse, have you considered your own body?<br>
<br>You the hallowed, holy, hearty Carcass<br>
<br>Called the sacred "Norton Anthology".<br>
<br>Looking through smoked glass believing it clear,<br>
<br>The panes of the Norton dimly show "truth";<br>
<br>Something so longed for; allusive as air.<br>
<br>From Frye's archetypical book of Books,<br>
<br>From whose panes English Culture<br>
<br>He said derives now its "origin, strength and core";<br>
<br>"What is Truth?" asked a now troubled solder;<br>
<br>He for all the Robe'd inquisitors;<br>
<br>Was this Pilate-without-oars given ear?<br>
<br>This record of their interrogation<br>
<br>Leaves English, perhaps all Human culture<br>
<br>Wanting - for a man gave him no answer.<br>
<br>"What is Truth?" For an answer came a<br>
<br>Silence that seems louder than we can hear,<br>
<br>Louder than at least you Norton can hear.<br>
<br>Thus for Milton, Woodsworth, Shelly or Blake,<br>
<br>If Western culture's "greatest influence"<br>
<br>Remained silent at the Soldier's request<br>
<br>How can robes of the honoured academy<br>
<br>Find answers from inferior minds who<br>
<br>"Through Eden took their Solitary Way"<br>
<br>Corpse, have you considered your own body?<br>
</p>
