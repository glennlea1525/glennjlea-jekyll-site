Glenn J Lea - Technical Writer
I'm a Canadian based in Berlin, Germany. My day job is a technical writer. In my spare time I write about Canadian and European history. I use this website mostly for a hands on way to teach myself the tools and techniques of building websites by hand without CMS tools. Read ny LinkedIn profile.

Projects

In Canada my work was mostly involved with financial software. Since moving to Berlin, I have focused mostly on wireless and telecommunications technologies:

  • Product documentation - mostly using MKDocs
  • Datasheets for wireless chips - Adobe Framemaker
  • Datasheets for circuit boards and related software
  • Documented software for eye tracking products (now Apple)
  • Manuals for using analytic software for big data
  • Endpoint protection hardware and software documentation
  • 2G/3G/4G mobile software documentation
  • Style guides and procedure manuals
  • Product illustrations, flyers, trade show banners and related collateral
  • Internal wikis and websites
  • Corporate websites

A (not) brief biography

I am from Toronto, Canada. Its no longer the sleepy and slow town it was when I grew up there. My family moved north to Richmond Hill, an even more sleepy town than Toronto. I made up for that by customizing muscle cars and racing them on the empty roads in the back country. In those days, these empty night-time streets were ideal race tracks to push the pedal to the metal and see what my cars could do (Plymouth GTXs). Eventually my car's insatiable appetite for fuel and parts led me to leave school to work in factories making good money - at least I thought it was.

After a few years of that and my parent's growing impatience with my loud car, I decided (with their blessing) to head west to Alberta at the tender age of 17 to start a new chapter in my life. So my buddy and I moved to the booming town of Calgary having only 150.00 CDN dollars between us. We managed the trip by coasting down any hill we encountered on the highways to save gas and survived on a bag full of cooked corn cobs, one block of Wisconsin cheese and a jug of orange juice. After a 52 hour non-stop drive, we arrived in Calgary and settled in at a friend's apartment. My buddy joined construction crews while I took odd jobs and made a valiant effort to finish my high school at an Adult vocational school. Having finished my high school, I went to work to make money for our trip back east. My claim to fame in Calgary was working for an oil-related company that made drill bits for oil rigs and was once owned by the famous Howard Hughes. It was fun work.

After three years I decided to move to the United States. Why? Because...well...that's what you do. Actually, I was invited to move to Lancaster, Pennsylvania by some friends and I had this crazy notion of wanting to take classes at Penn State University. I really enjoyed living in Lancaster, PA. Its a nice town surrounded by Amish farms and I frequently saw horse and buggies trotting up major roads on the way to markets. Not being able to afford Penn State, I started attending a local college (Millersville State College) taking evening computer science courses. It was pay as you go. Work all day. Study in the evening. Find some time to sleep. Repeat next day, and so on. My first computer language I learned was Fortran, specifically WATFIV Fortran from the University of Waterloo. That was fun.

After a year or so, I moved on to Rhode Island to live by the Atlantic coast. I was fortunate to learn great New England carpentry. I learned how to install cedar shingle roofs and walls and renovate turn of the century seaside homes. And during my time off I got to enjoy the waters of the Atlantic Ocean in Westerly, Rhode Island. But I hadn't forgotten about my university goals. I did manage to attend evening classes at the University of Rhode Island. I kept at it as best I could moving it to a slower pace to keep food on the table.

After six years I returned to Canada and managed somehow - both my parent's and my surprise - to enter full-time at the University of Waterloo in Ontario. The city of Waterloo turned out to be quite similar to Lancaster except that the horse and buggies were owned by Old Order Mennonites rather than Amish. In fact, some of my favourite professors were Mennonite or associated with Mennonite churches. Initially, my plan was to become a high-school teacher so I studied English and History and minored in Psychology. However, during my last year I discovered teachers were having a hard time finding work. After looking around I discovered technical writing. This seemed like a good choice for a career. I loved to write and to teach so this career path fit in with my goals. So, beginning in 1994 I started my career at Citibank Canada in Toronto as a technical writer documenting trading systems. I've been at this profession ever since and it turned out to be a better choice than teaching high-school. Teaching adults to use new products is far more challenging and rewarding.

About this website

This website is another in a long line of websites I have created over the years, mostly as learning exercises. I do this for fun. I made my first website some time during 1998 using a text editor and was called something like WebWonderWorld (someone actually owns a domain with this name - who knew!). This got me hooked into making these things. It isn't my main focus but it is fun to do. I write product documentation not develop websites. But as product documentation is mostly delivered now as websites, all this experience with HTML, Markdown and CSS has become handy.

Having built websites using all sorts of different tools, I scrapped all CMSs like Wordpress and the like and built this one as a static website. I first began by gathering in one place all my domains which were scattered around at various hosting services. Then I took a deep dive into Jekyll, Markdown, Liquid and responsive layouts and went at it. I am sure this will change in time but for now, I will stick with this technology for the time being.
